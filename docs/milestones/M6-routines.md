Why (Steve, 2026-09-24): "AI could make them do an unprogrammed routine just based upon my questions, is that possible? If that happens, AI should keep checking in and steering the NPC if needed until it is done."

## Caps and budget
- Steps in a plan: 8. Check-ins a routine: 5. Routines a person: 2 (one running, one waiting its turn, checked again when it starts). Routines in the town: 4, running and waiting.
- Time: its own estimate (straight metres x 1.5 for the streets x 2.4 game minutes a metre walked in view, plus waits, plus 120), never under 240 nor over 900 game minutes. "Two game hours" is 40 real seconds here, and a walk of 100 m in Jef's sight takes about four game hours; 900 game minutes is five real minutes. A changed way adds its own time within the cap. Time up: they give it up, the coins and things come back.
- Budget: `ROUTINE_CALLS_PER_DAY = 6` in `server/src/config.ts` (hook `routine_checkin`), from what was left, never the reserve. The shares now come to 96 of 120, leaving 24 for the board, the outcomes, the named people and the epilogue (the last 15 the reserve). The plan itself rides in the talk call. `routine_checkin` is in `PLAYER_TEXT_HOOKS`: it only goes to Claude.
- Check-ins cost a call only when something is off (a failed step, the rain, the night). A step that went as planned is looked at by the engine alone. With six a day, a check-in after every step would use the day's share on one errand.

## Not yet
- Check-ins run when something is off, not after every step (the budget; see above). A long watch is not asked about the rain until it ends.
- Shops have no stock, so "sold out" never happens; a shut place stands in for it.
- When the steps run out the action row says "done" even when the errand failed; the `errand_done` / `errand_failed` event says which.
- The model sometimes plans a give and a message to the same person; both are played.
- Runners are honest: the hired hands' dishonest roll (walking off, asking for more) is not applied to errands.
- `enter` is played as walking to the door; nobody goes into a room on an errand.
- Townspeople hold no things in the database: what they carry lives in the routine; the addressee keeps a memory of it.
- The crowd's walker can wedge a person against a wall or a stall after an unseen walk (seen near the Grote Markt); errands squeeze out, the other routines (the hands, the treat) do not.
- Found on the way, not mine: M4's start text for a talk_to shows a bare id ("Ward Goossens went to have a word with r147.", `actions.ts describeStart`).
