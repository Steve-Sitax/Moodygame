# M6 - Emigrants on the Rijnkaai, 2026-09-24

Why: Steve, 2026-09-24, "implement your 10 suggestions"; this one was: "Emigrants. Families with bundles wait on the quay and in cheap lodging houses for the new Red Star Line ship."

## The history we used (research, with sources)
- **Where they came from.** In the 1870s and 1880s the rail links sent many emigrants from **Switzerland and western and southern Germany** through Antwerp [2]. German mass emigration began in the south-west (Baden, Wurttemberg, the Palatinate, Hesse) [3]. Switzerland sent about 35,000 overseas emigrants in each of the 1860s and 1870s, many from Bern and Zurich; home villages sometimes paid part of the fare to lighten their poor relief [7][8]. Few Belgians left in the 1870s (about 1,300 reached the US in 1873), from Luxembourg province, round Wavre and from Flanders; most went to Wisconsin, Michigan and Illinois [9][10]. Dutch, Bohemian and Hungarian emigrants through Antwerp in 1873: no reliable source found. The game has them as a small share only.
- **Why they left.** Low wages, farm distress, men wanting land of their own, military service (after 1870-71 the south German states served on the Prussian model: 7 years in the army, then 5 in the Landwehr), and "letters and remittances of friends and relations" [4][5]. Letters from Wisconsin read out on the church steps set off Belgian rushes [11].
- **Antwerp in 1873.** The port had almost stopped carrying emigrants: in 1854 about 24,000 left, and by 1871 none. From 1873 the Red Star Line's regular steamers brought the trade back [1]. So a mixed first-year crowd of a few families at a time fits the history better than a packed quay.
- **Red Star Line.** The International Navigation Company was chartered in Pennsylvania on 5 May 1871. Its Belgian company, the Societe Anonyme de Navigation Belge-Americaine, was set up in September 1872, with Von der Becke and Marsily as directors [13][14][1]. The **Vaderland** left Antwerp on 20 January 1873 for Philadelphia. She ran short of coal, put in at Halifax, and reached Philadelphia on 17 February [1]. The **Nederland** made her first voyage in November 1873 [15]. Chartered ships filled the gaps [16]. The Pennsylvania Railroad financed the line and carried passengers inland from Philadelphia [1]. The ships lay at the **Rijnkaai**, the railway's quay by the Rhine goods station [1][18]. I found no evidence of lighters for Red Star emigrants. In the game the liner lies at anchor and lighters take the emigrants out (world/anchorage.ts, the tides helper's design). That part is our story.
- **Lodging, agents, runners.** Early emigrant lodging was cramped houses by the harbour, with "extortion and arbitrariness"; station hotels came only after 1880 [19]. The lodging houses of the Schipperskwartier cost about 3 francs a day on main streets and 10-12 francs a week in side streets; the keepers were mostly Belgian, with Germans among them [20]. The worst abuse reported was **false tickets for travel inside the US** [9]. An 1846 emigrant warned against "every German thieving trickster approaching you as exchange agent" [21]. Sub-agents needed no licence until 1876 [9]. Uniformed Red Star staff meeting the trains came only in the 1880s [1].
- **What they carried.** Steerage passengers brought their own mattress, bedding, plate, mug and cutlery, and packed "stout boxes, well roped" [22]. Letters home advised ham, butter, coffee, boots, even a waffle iron, with the chests tightly bound [21]. Belgian railways carried German emigrants' baggage free from 1843 [9]. The featherbeds are traditional but not sourced for Antwerp.
- **The inspection.** Belgium's first emigrant regulation was the royal decree of 14 March 1843. J.-F. Thielens was emigration inspector from 1846 for about thirty years, so probably still in office in 1873. A supervision commission (royal decree of 19 March 1855) checked the ships' food before departure. Medical checks were superficial until about 1892 [9][19][23]. **Not in the game yet** (see Not yet).
- **Railway and waiting.** Antwerp has had a rail link to Cologne since 1843; in 1873 the trains came into Antwerpen-Oost [19][24]. The families waited "a few days" [19]. The exact wait for 1873 is not sourced.

## For main: the director (not edited; server/src/director/* belongs to M4b)
The emigrant_ship template and my boarding are independent now. It all works without director changes. To tie them together:
1. `templates.ts` emigrant_ship `fits`: `(d, h) => isShipDay(d) && h >= 8 && h < 16` (import `isShipDay` from `../town/emigrants.ts`). The event then comes on the days the families really go.
2. `scheduler.ts fits()`: role `"family"` becomes `isEmigrant(r) && emigrantShip(db).households.includes(r.household)`, so the event gathers a family that is really leaving. **All other roles** should skip `isEmigrant(r)`: otherwise a wedding may call emigrants away from their chests. While an event holds a family, the client waits and boards them after it releases them.
3. A proper hook for the lighters: the client reads `window.__anchorage.tows()` and `.hulls()` (the anchorage's dev object, read only). A `world.anchorage()` accessor with the same two functions would be cleaner (anchorage.ts belongs to the tides helper).
4. Optional: `emigrantShip(db)` (ship's day, boarding now, the households going today, camp and berth points) is ready for the director's prompt.
