## Research: the Poesje around 1870
- Antwerp's puppet theatre with rod puppets ("poesjenellen", from Pulcinella), played in cellars in the working quarters, Sint-Andries and the Schipperskwartier. The best known opened in 1862 in a cellar in the Repenstraat, by the Vleeshuis ("Poesje van de Reep"); it still plays there. Our cellar door is opposite the Vleeshuis for that reason.
- The puppets: rod puppets of 50 to 60 cm, wooden heads, forearms and legs, an iron rod to the head and one to the right hand. Stock figures: De Neus (the big nose, the lead), De Schele (cross-eyed, stammering, wise), De Kop, De Bult, Belleke.
- The audience: dockers, sailors, the poor and their children; players and public both of the lower class. Plays were improvised in Antwerp dialect round a plot, full of local references (the players asked about the audience beforehand), often serials, ending in a big fight.
- The price: "a few cents" to go down into the cellar. No exact 1870s price found; 5 centimes is our choice.
- The dice: pitjesbak is a Flemish tavern dice game with three dice (ace 100, six 60, special throws). Our rules are a simplified one-throw version; whether the name was in use in 1873 is not checked.
- Sources: [Poesje (theater), Wikipedia NL](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poesje_(theater)); [Antwerpse Poesje, Erfgoedbibliotheek Hendrik Conscience](https://consciencebibliotheek.be/nl/pagina/antwerpse-poesje); [De Poesjenellenkelder, Open Monumentendag 2023, stad Antwerpen](https://magazine.antwerpen.be/open-monumentendag-2023/de-poesjenellenkelder); [World Encyclopedia of Puppetry Arts, Belgium](https://wepa.unima.org/en/belgium/); the "few cents" from an aantwaarpe.be article on "Klein maar Moedig" (search snippet; the page did not load).
